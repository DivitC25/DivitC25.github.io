I started with finding the ideal colorizing shifts of low-res JPEGs consisting of the cathedral, the monastery, and the tobolsk using single-scale alignment. In order to try a good shift search radius and not hugely inflate the processing time per image, I tried every offset in a ±15 window for both x and y. I scored each candidate on its similarity to the blue plate utilizing the NCC (effectively normalized cosine similarity) metric, while also implementing the L2 scoring metric for comparisons.

I picked NCC because its normalization of both images relative to their respective brightness level allows an apples-to-apples comparison. In line with the recommendations in the assignment description, I only performed post flattening NCC scoring on overlapped regions of both images to remove shift padding. Before this scoring, I did a 25% border crop to remove high-contrast plate margins and handwritten marks that would otherwise dominate NCC. I also utilized sobel edges in scoring instead of raw intensity to highlight shared geometric features (windows, rooflines, railings) and downweight brightness differences that do not indicate features.

With this pipeline the cathedral, monastery, and tobolsk come out sharp in the subject area with no color ghosting. Notably, the colored bands at the frame edges represent uncropped plate borders, not misalignment.

I then moved on to high-resolution TIF plates, for which a single ±15 search is too narrow a search space to detect true displacements and too slow when applied to full-resolution images. In order to make the search feasible, I created an image pyramid by downsampling the channels by a factor of two repeatedly until the longest dimension became smaller than about 300 pixels and implemented a coarse-to-fine approach. On the coarsest scale, ±30 windows in both x and y directions were checked, while on each subsequent level the optimal shift found was doubled, and further refinement performed within a narrower ±5 window, allowing to find large displacements without checking all pixels in the whole image.

I utilized the exact same scoring pipeline tweaks for this task as the single scale processing. I picked L2 scoring for this task as it proved to be the most stable across all of the high-resolution images.

The NCC measure was kept as a backup option in case of SSD failure. If there is a significant difference between the red/blue shift and green/blue shift or the red/blue shift itself is higher than a certain threshold, the image is realigned using NCC to overcome possible failure of the algorithm on a spurious peak; this method helped to correct the images like “icon,” in which SSD found an extremely wrong displacement.

With this coarse-to-fine approach, the high-resolution plates become sharp in the subject area, having little or no color ghosting at all. It is worth noting that the colored lines at the edges of the frames are uncropped plate borders and not misalignment.
